Zhang Daqian (1899--1983) is the case that unsettles everything taught so
far. A celebrated painter in his own right, he was also, by his own
cheerful admission, one of the most successful forgers of the twentieth
century: paintings he produced after the seventeenth-century masters Shitao
and Bada Shanren were bought as genuine by major museums, including, by its
own later account, the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston. He didn't hide this. At
a 1967 exhibition of Shitao paintings he is reported to have laughed his way
down the wall, pointing out which ones he had painted himself.

Zhang forged as a scholar-connoisseur, not a copyist: his forgeries drew on
collections of the very artists he imitated, which he had assembled and
studied for years, and he sometimes improved on his sources rather than
merely reproducing them --- swapping in a more prestigious collector's seal
where the original bore an obscure one, in at least one documented case. If
forgery this considered can also be genuine scholarship and genuine
craft, the tidy line this course has assumed between "real" and "fake" gets
harder to hold, and week 11 is what happens when you stop assuming it.

## Outline

- Zhang Daqian's career as painter, collector and forger, in that order
- specific forgeries that fooled documented experts and institutions
- what his own attitude to his forgeries --- open, unashamed, often proud
  --- implies about forgery as a craft tradition
- the ethical question this leaves open going into week 11
