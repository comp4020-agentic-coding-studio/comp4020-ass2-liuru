# The Tang Yin Problem

Week 1 --- Why Tang Yin

---

## A painter forged in his own lifetime

Tang Yin (1470--1524), Suzhou, Ming dynasty

- workshops in his own city forged his signature and seals while he was alive
- the practice didn't stop when he died --- it ran for two more centuries
- the broad label for it: **Suzhou pian**, "Suzhou fakes"

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Some "Suzhou pian" pieces were good enough to later enter the Qing
> imperial collection as genuine.

---

## Nobody agrees on a number

- his surviving attributed oeuvre: roughly 100--200 paintings, worldwide
- how many are actually his hand: contested, painting by painting
- this course doesn't try to settle the number --- it teaches you the
  instruments the field uses to argue each case

---

## Why one painter, all semester

A course that jumped between ten painters would teach ten shallow case
studies. Tang Yin's afterlife contains almost every kind of evidence this
field has:

- contested seals
- forged colophons
- attributions that have flipped in print, more than once
- a real, ongoing scholarly argument, not a settled textbook fact

---

## The semester's shape

1. **Weeks 1--6** --- build the toolkit: materials, brushwork, seals, colophons
2. **Weeks 7--8** --- the market, and a real case study
3. **Weeks 9--10** --- complicate the toolkit: computation, the forger's own craft
4. **Weeks 11--12** --- what any of this is for, and a defended verdict
